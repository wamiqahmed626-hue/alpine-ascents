Technical Mountaineering Techniques

Essential skills that every mountain climber must practice until instinctual

Fundamentals

Core Alpine Skills

These fundamentals outline theoretical concepts. Field practice requires qualified alpine instructors.

Skill 01

Knots & Tying In

The figure-eight follow-through knot is the universal standard for tying directly into the climbing harness. The clove hitch and prusik friction hitch are essential for anchors and rope ascension.

Skill 02

Belaying & Anchoring

Managing the rope through an approved belay device to catch an unexpected partner slip. Anchors must be redundant, equalized, and multidirectional in rock, snow, or solid ice.

Skill 03

Crampon Technique

Flat-footing (French technique) maintains maximum spike contact on moderate snow slopes. Front-pointing (German technique) enables climbing vertical blue ice with calf strength.

Skill 04

Ice Axe Handling

Used as a walking cane (piolet canne) for balance, an anchor for belaying, a dagger in firm snow, and an essential tool for cutting clean steps in icy traverses.

Skill 05

Self-Arrest Procedure

The immediate reflex of driving the ice axe pick into the snowpack and kicking toe points up to stop an unroped fall before picking up deadly speed on an icy slope.

Skill 06

Rappelling & Descent

Descending fixed ropes using a belay device backed up with an autoblock knot. Rappel accidents account for significant alpine casualties — both rope ends must be knotted.

Glacier Travel

Traversing Crevassed Icefields

Glaciers are constantly moving rivers of ice riddled with deep chasms called crevasses, often concealed beneath fragile seasonal snow bridges.

  • - Rope up with 10-15 meters of taut spacing between team members.
  • - Probe questionable snow ahead with ski poles or an inverted axe shaft.
  • - Carry prusiks, pulleys, and snow pickets ready for hauling systems (Z-pulley).
  • - Travel during early morning freeze before solar radiation softens bridges.
