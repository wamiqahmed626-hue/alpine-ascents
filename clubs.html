Global Directory

Mountaineering Organizations

Club Name Headquarters Founded Primary Focus & Notes
The Alpine Club London, United Kingdom 1857 The world's first mountaineering club; publishes the Alpine Journal.
Club Alpino Italiano (CAI) Milan, Italy 1863 Maintains hundreds of mountain refuges (rifugi) across the Italian Alps and Dolomites.
Swiss Alpine Club (SAC) Bern, Switzerland 1863 Operates mountain huts, mountaineering training programs, and search-and-rescue support.
Deutscher Alpenverein (DAV) Munich, Germany 1869 Largest climbing association in the world; manages climbing centers, huts, and alpine safety research.
American Alpine Club (AAC) Golden, Colorado, USA 1902 Supports conservation, climbing grants, library archives, and publishes Accidents in North American Climbing.
The Himalayan Club Mumbai, India 1928 Encourages exploration in the Himalaya, Karakoram, and Hindu Kush ranges.
Nepal Mountaineering Association (NMA) Kathmandu, Nepal 1973 National alpine body managing trekking peak permits and mountaineering training for Sherpas.
Alpine Club of Pakistan (ACP) Islamabad, Pakistan 1974 National body for mountaineering, rock climbing, and expeditions across the Karakoram, Himalaya, and Hindu Kush.
Geography

Great Mountain Ranges of the World

The Alps

Spanning France, Switzerland, Italy, and Austria. The historical birthplace of alpine mountaineering, home to Mont Blanc and the Matterhorn.

The Himalaya

Home to Mount Everest and the highest concentration of 8,000-meter peaks on Earth, stretching across Nepal, India, Bhutan, and Pakistan.

The Karakoram

Located in northern Pakistan, home to K2, Broad Peak, the Gasherbrums, and immense non-polar glaciers including the Baltoro.

The Andes

The longest continental mountain range on Earth, stretching along South America's western spine. Summit peak: Aconcagua (6,961 m).

Alaska Range

Rugged, glaciated, and exposed to sub-Arctic weather systems. Dominated by Denali (6,190 m), the highest peak in North America.

East Africa

Volcanic summits rising above equatorial plains: Mount Kilimanjaro (5,895 m) and Mount Kenya (5,199 m) with unique high-altitude zones.
