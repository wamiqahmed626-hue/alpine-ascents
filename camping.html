Camping & Sheltering

Rest, hydration, and high-altitude shelter craft in severe alpine conditions

Site Selection

Where to Pitch Camp

Choosing where to pitch a tent in high mountains requires analyzing objective terrain hazards long before night falls.

Avoid Hazard Runouts

Never camp directly beneath active avalanche gullies, hanging seracs, or loose rock cliffs.

Wind Protection & Snow Walls

Seek natural windbreaks or construct 3-foot snow block walls on the windward side of your tent.

Stamped Platforms

Stomp down snow with boots or snowshoes and let it freeze solid for 30 minutes before staking tents with deadman anchors.

Clean Melting Snow

Designate a clean uphill snow quarry exclusively for melting water to prevent camp contamination.

Shelter Options

Methods of High-Altitude Shelter

4-Season Tent

Geodesic aluminium pole structures engineered with rugged breathable nylon fabrics to endure 60-knot gale winds and heavy snowfall loads.

Bivouac (Bivy)

A waterproof and breathable outer sack slipped directly over your sleeping bag. Ideal for lightweight summit pushes or emergency cliff-side ledges.

Snow Cave

Excavated into a deep snowbank. It maintains an interior temperature near 0°C even when outside storms rage at -30°C. Must have a ventilation hole.

Mountain Huts

Permanent stone or timber alpine refuges managed by alpine clubs throughout Europe, America, and Asia providing dry dormitories and meals.

Carbon Monoxide Warning

Never operate a camp stove inside a tightly sealed tent or enclosed snow cave without adequate cross-ventilation. Toxic carbon monoxide gas is invisible, odorless, and rapidly fatal at high altitudes where oxygen levels are already low.
