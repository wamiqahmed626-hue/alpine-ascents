History of Mountaineering

A timeline of landmark ascents and explorations that shaped high-altitude climbing

Chronology

From Alps to Karakoram

Mountaineering grew from scientific curiosity in the 18th-century European Alps into a global pursuit across the Himalayas, Andes, and Karakoram.

Sherpa & Local Guides

Many of the greatest historic ascents relied on the courage and route-finding of local Sherpa and Pakistani high-altitude porters.

1786 - Mont Blanc First Ascent

Jacques Balmat & Michel-Gabriel Paccard

Balmat and Paccard reached the summit of Western Europe's highest peak (4,809 m), which is widely marked as the birth of modern mountaineering.

1857 - Foundation of The Alpine Club

The World's First Mountaineering Club

Established in London, the Alpine Club pioneered structured mountain exploration, hut construction, and published route journals.

1865 - The Matterhorn Tragedy & Triumph

Edward Whymper's Ascent

Whymper's team conquered the iconic Matterhorn, but four climbers tragically died during descent when their rope snapped.

1938 - Eiger North Face Conquered

The Heckmair Expedition

A combined German-Austrian team completed the first ascent of the notorious Nordwand (North Face) of the Eiger in Switzerland.

1950 - First 8,000-Metre Peak

Annapurna I (8,091 m)

Maurice Herzog and Louis Lachenal led a French expedition to summit Annapurna I, proving that human beings could survive at extreme altitudes.

1953 - Mount Everest First Ascent

Tenzing Norgay & Sir Edmund Hillary

On May 29, 1953, Sherpa Tenzing Norgay and Edmund Hillary stepped onto the highest point on Earth (8,848 m) via the South Col route.

1978 - Everest Without Bottled Oxygen

Reinhold Messner & Peter Habeler

Messner and Habeler shattered the physiological belief that climbing Everest without bottled oxygen was lethal, opening a new era of pure alpine-style ascents.

1986 - Crown of the Himalaya Completed

All 14 Eight-Thousanders

Reinhold Messner became the first person in history to climb all 14 peaks above 8,000 meters across Nepal, Pakistan, and China.

2021 - K2 First Winter Ascent

Historic Nepali Climbers Team

A team of ten Nepali mountaineers, including Nirmal Purja and Mingma G, conquered K2 (8,611 m) in harsh winter conditions for the first time in human history.
